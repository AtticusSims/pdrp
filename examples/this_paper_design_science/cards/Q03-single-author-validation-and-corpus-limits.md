# Q03: Single-author validation bounds, bias-test statistical power, and N=4 multidisciplinary corpus scope

Four methodological scope boundaries qualify the human validation and cross-disciplinary generalization claims (article:167, 270-286, 327-332; supplement_s1:146-179). First, both Task 1 (68/68 crate audit) and Task 2 (180 blinded calibration items) were executed by the study's sole author, with stopwatch timing recorded on the two authored internal manuscripts (17.4 min and 12.6 min, mean 15.0 min) and unlogged self-reported estimates (~15 min each) on the two external eLife companions audited as a non-author reader. Multi-rater studies with independent original authors and blind domain raters remain essential. Second, in Task 2, all 180 stratified items received full blind clean-success pass/fail and binary error-flag ratings, while optional per-criterion checkboxes were logged on 49/180 items (supplement_s1:161-162). Third, while two-sided Fisher exact condition-bias tests were non-significant across all judges (p = 0.18 to 1.00), sample sizes (72 companion vs. 46 raw trials in the disagreement-enriched sample) lack power to rule out small 5-10 pp bias shifts. Fourth, four multidisciplinary papers (N = 4) cannot exhaust all scholarly genres; multimodal figures, interactive code notebooks, and CAD repositories remain untested, and regional commercial API policies restricted the cloud fleet to five globally accessible families.
